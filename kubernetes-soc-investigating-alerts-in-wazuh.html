Investigation objective

Practice alert triage. The goal is not to click randomly through a dashboard. The goal is to answer a repeatable set of questions with evidence.

Alert review checklist

  • When did the alert occur?
  • What source or agent generated it?
  • What rule fired?
  • What severity was assigned?
  • What does the description claim happened?
  • What does the full log show?
  • Is this expected lab activity?
  • What action is recommended?

Kubernetes correlation commands

kubectl get pods -A -o wide
kubectl get events -A --sort-by=.lastTimestamp
kubectl logs -n soc-lab <pod-name>
kubectl describe pod -n soc-lab <pod-name>

Decision outcomes

  • Expected lab activity: document and close as test.
  • Benign but noisy: create a tuning note.
  • Misconfiguration: document a remediation finding.
  • Suspicious and unexplained: escalate as an incident candidate.
Analyst note: A good investigation explains why you believe something, not just what the dashboard displayed.

Check your understanding

Use these questions to check whether the lab and analyst concepts are clear before moving forward.

  1. What is the first goal of triage?
  2. Why review the full log?
  3. Why correlate with Kubernetes commands?
  4. What is a rule ID useful for?
  5. What should you do with expected lab activity?
  6. What should you do with noisy benign activity?
  7. What makes something an incident candidate?
  8. Why document evidence?
  9. Why is severity not enough by itself?
  10. What does a good investigation explain?
Answer key
  1. Understand what happened and whether action is needed.
  2. It contains underlying evidence/details.
  3. To validate source, workload, and timing.
  4. Finding rule details, repeat alerts, and tuning discussions.
  5. Document and close as test.
  6. Create a tuning note.
  7. Suspicious, unexplained activity with possible impact or risk.
  8. To support conclusions and follow-up.
  9. Severity needs context, source, and evidence.
  10. Why the conclusion is reasonable.
