Containment in a Kubernetes lab

In a safe lab, containment may mean scaling down a demo deployment, isolating a namespace with NetworkPolicy, removing a test pod, rotating a demo secret, or stopping a test workload. Do not perform destructive actions on real systems without an approved response process.

Incident note: Avoid vague claims like “Kubernetes was hacked.” Say what alert fired, what workload was involved, what evidence exists, and what action is recommended.

Check your understanding

Use these questions to check whether the lab and analyst concepts are clear before moving forward.

  1. What is triage?
  2. What is scoping?
  3. Why collect evidence before action?
  4. What is impact assessment?
  5. What is containment?
  6. What is remediation?
  7. Why include detection follow-up?
  8. Why should communication avoid vague claims?
  9. What is a lab-safe containment action?
  10. Why not use this lab as a real incident plan by itself?
Answer key
  1. Determining what the alert means and what action is needed.
  2. Determining affected systems, users, namespaces, workloads, and timeframe.
  3. To support decisions and avoid guessing.
  4. Determining business/technical effect.
  5. Limiting further activity or exposure.
  6. Fixing the underlying issue.
  7. To improve future alert quality.
  8. Vague claims create confusion and poor decisions.
  9. Scaling down or deleting a demo workload, or isolating a lab namespace.
  10. Real incidents require approved organizational processes.
